## 2. The generated set (one consistent style)

See `icon_set.png` (Documents · Evidence · Retrieval) and `hero_texture.png`. All share one line style — navy `#1F3864` strokes, a single green `#2F6F4E` accent, rounded caps — matching my Identity Kit. A set, not a pile.

## 3. Where I chose a real capture over AI (the calls)

- **DecisAI proof:** real product screenshots over any generated "AI app" visual — a system built to refuse hallucination cannot be represented by a hallucinated image.
- **ML work:** real executed-notebook outputs (the actual leakage-jump numbers) over a generated chart — the honesty of the number is the point.
- **About:** a real photo of me over an AI portrait — trust starts with being a real person.

## 4. What I rejected, and why (the discernment part)

**Rejected: a generated "glowing AI brain / neural-network hero" background.** It's the default AI-portfolio cliché — blue circuitry, glowing nodes, a floating brain. I rejected it for three concrete reasons, not taste:

1. **It contradicts my claim.** My whole pitch is that my systems *don't* invent things and stay grounded in real evidence. A flashy, generated, meaning-free hero says the opposite about how I work.
2. **It competes with the work.** My style note says the design stays quiet so the work is loudest. A glowing hero is the loudest thing on the page — it steals attention from the real DecisAI screenshots that actually prove something.
3. **It could describe anyone.** A neural-brain hero sits on ten thousand AI portfolios. My identity is supposed to be a thing only I could show — real captures of real work do that; a stock-AI hero erases it.

The calm dot-grid texture I kept does the one job a hero background actually needs (a little depth) without any of those costs.
